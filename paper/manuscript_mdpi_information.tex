\Title{Prequential Answer-Set Calibration for Temporal Knowledge Graph Forecasting: An ICEWS14 Diagnostic Study}

\Author{Xinyu Wang $^{1,*}$}
\AuthorNames{Xinyu Wang}
\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Anhui Institute of Information Technology, Wuhu 241000, China}
\corres{Correspondence: xywang68@iflytek.com}

\abstract{Temporal knowledge graph (TKG) forecasting is usually evaluated by ranking accuracy, but downstream systems may need candidate answer sets whose empirical coverage remains reliable as the stream evolves. We evaluate a leakage-controlled prequential calibration protocol on ICEWS14. A continuous-time DistMult scorer is frozen after training; at each test timestamp, all subject and object queries are predicted before labels are revealed, and calibration pools are updated only after the complete timestamp batch. Across five seeds and controlled training-history deletion up to 30\%, static empirical calibration under-covers the later stream: at 30\% deletion, observed-label coverage is 0.8235 against a 0.90 target. Equal-weight rolling calibration reaches 0.8998, with paired timestamp-block undercoverage reduction 0.0723 (95\% CI [0.0535, 0.0938]). This reliability repair is costly: rolling calibration returns 3,802.5 entities on average from a 7,128-entity vocabulary, and 36.5\% of unique-query sets equal the full vocabulary. Rank-threshold rolling reduces mean size to 3,668.2 with observed-label coverage 0.8992, but full-set query coverage decreases to 0.8881. Validation-selected RAPS rolling, chosen on calibration-only validation batches from a fixed APS/RAPS grid, selects k=50 and lambda=0.0001 with tolerance 0.02 and reaches coverage 0.8987 with mean size 3,319.0, but it does not dominate rank thresholding in the tail or at target 0.92. Relation-side, delayed-feedback, window, and set-size diagnostics support a bounded conclusion: rolling prequential calibration improves observed-label reliability while exposing a substantial reliability--utility trade-off.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge graphs represent relational facts as edges between entities, but many
facts are valid only at particular times. Temporal knowledge graphs augment
relational facts with timestamps and support forecasting queries such as
\emph{which entity will participate in relation \(r\) with subject \(s\) at
time \(t\)?} A growing family of models encodes temporal evolution through
time-aware factorization, recurrent graph networks, or local--global history
aggregation \cite{garciaduran2018sequence,li2021regcn,li2022tirgn,cai2023survey}.
Their standard evaluation reports filtered mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and
Hits@\(K\). These metrics are indispensable for measuring ordering quality, but
they do not answer a deployment question: when the model is uncertain, how many
candidate entities must a downstream system retain to cover an observed correct
answer at a target rate?

This distinction matters because ranking and reliability are not equivalent.
A model can rank the true entity near the top while assigning scores whose scale
changes substantially across relations or time. Conversely, a system may obtain
high empirical coverage by returning a set containing a large fraction of the
entity vocabulary. Prior work has shown that confidence calibration for static
knowledge graph embeddings depends strongly on the evaluation assumption
\cite{safavi2020calibration}. More recently, conformalized knowledge graph
embedding methods have constructed answer sets with marginal coverage under
exchangeability, and predicate-conditional variants have strengthened
reliability across relation groups \cite{zhu2025kgcp,zhu2025condkgcp}. These
advances show that answer-set uncertainty is useful for knowledge graph
reasoning, while temporal deployment remains less studied.

In TKG forecasting, calibration examples precede test queries in time.
Distributional changes in event frequencies, participating entities, recurrence
patterns, and score geometry violate the symmetry that supports classical split
conformal prediction. The problem becomes more severe when the training graph
does not contain all relevant historical facts. A scorer trained on incomplete
history can change both its ranking and its nonconformity-score distribution.
Accordingly, a fixed threshold estimated once on an early calibration period may
under-cover later windows, while a naively expanding threshold may achieve
coverage only by returning unusably large sets.

The temporal graph literature also cautions against attributing progress to
complexity alone. A recurrence-based method can be competitive with substantially
more elaborate TKG forecasters on several benchmarks
\cite{gastinger2024repeat}, and TGB~2.0 reports both competitive heuristic
baselines and scalability failures on large temporal graphs
\cite{gastinger2024tgb2}. Reliability studies should therefore retain simple
Frequency and Repeat baselines, audit temporal leakage, and report the cost of
calibration rather than focusing only on a favorable coverage number.

We study these issues through \emph{prequential empirical calibration}. At each
timestamp, all subject and object queries are predicted using a threshold chosen
before their labels are available. Only after the complete timestamp batch has
been scored are its observed labels added to the calibration history. The main
comparison is between static calibration and recent-history prequential
calibration. We also report fixed recency weighting, an exploratory half-life
selector, and shortlist variants as diagnostics rather than as the primary
contribution.

The paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We formulate TKG answer-set calibration under temporal drift and
  controlled history deletion, separating ranking, label coverage,
  multi-answer semantics, and prediction-set cost.
  \item We implement a deterministic, model-agnostic timestamp-batched protocol
  for static, rolling, fixed recency-weighted, and selected half-life
  calibration without current-timestamp labels.
  \item We define a leakage-controlled experimental split with disjoint temporal
  roles, complete-batch updates, four controlled history-deletion rates, five
  seeds, and paired uncertainty summaries over seeds and timestamps.
  \item We show in a five-seed ICEWS14 run that rolling prequential calibration
  repairs static undercoverage but produces large sets, and we quantify
  rank-based, APS/RAPS-based, relation-side, set-size, window, and
  delayed-feedback trade-offs rather than hiding negative diagnostics.
\end{itemize}

The scope is empirical. Exponential age weights are not density ratios, and the
weighted quantile does not include the test-point mass used in formal weighted
conformal methods. We therefore attribute guarantees only to baselines under
their stated assumptions.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Temporal Knowledge Graph Forecasting}

Early temporal completion approaches enriched static factorization with
time-aware relation representations \cite{garciaduran2018sequence}. RE-GCN
models the evolution of entity and relation representations through recurrent
relation-aware graph convolution \cite{li2021regcn}, while TiRGN combines local
recurrent structure with global repetitive historical patterns
\cite{li2022tirgn}. These methods illustrate a broader division between models
that extrapolate from recent graph states, models that exploit recurrence, and
models that learn continuous or discrete time representations
\cite{cai2023survey}. The calibration study in this paper is not a replacement
for these forecasters. It is a post-hoc evaluation layer that consumes candidate
scores and can therefore be used to test whether reliability behavior transfers
across backbones.

Evaluation is especially important in this area. Gastinger \emph{et al.}
showed that a simple recurrence baseline ranked first or third on three of five
datasets in a comparison with eleven methods \cite{gastinger2024repeat}. TGB~2.0
extended temporal graph benchmarking to larger multi-relational datasets and
found that simple heuristics remain competitive while many methods fail to run
at the largest scale \cite{gastinger2024tgb2}. We adopt the same lesson: a
calibration method is not compelling if its underlying scorer is weaker than a
frequency or recurrence heuristic, or if exact candidate scoring is
computationally infeasible.

Continual TKG studies further emphasize that histories evolve and that retaining
all earlier facts is not a neutral assumption
\cite{mirtaheri2023continual,zhang2025replay}. Our controlled deletions are not
presented as a complete model of real missingness. Instead, they provide
reproducible interventions for measuring how scorer degradation and threshold
staleness interact.

\subsection{Calibration for Knowledge Graphs}

Probability calibration maps model confidence to empirical correctness.
Temperature scaling and related post-hoc methods can improve neural
classification calibration \cite{guo2017calibration}; however, KGE link
prediction differs from ordinary closed-set classification because unobserved
facts are not necessarily false. Safavi \emph{et al.} found that KGE
calibration is substantially more difficult under an open-world evaluation than
under a closed-world assumption \cite{safavi2020calibration}.

KGCP constructs conformal answer sets for link-prediction queries and reports
that the resulting sets meet the target marginal coverage under its experimental
setting \cite{zhu2025kgcp}. CondKGCP extends this direction toward
predicate-conditional behavior by grouping related predicates and augmenting
calibration with rank information \cite{zhu2025condkgcp}. Our problem differs
from their static setting in three ways. First, examples are ordered in time and are evaluated strictly
after the data used for calibration. Second, the calibration state is updated
only after complete timestamp batches. Third, the scorer itself is stressed by
controlled deletion of training history. KGCP and CondKGCP remain essential
baselines, but their assumptions and guarantees do not apply directly to a
temporally ordered prequential evaluation.

\subsection{Conformal Prediction under Distribution Shift}

Classical conformal prediction constructs prediction sets under exchangeability
\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}. Weighted conformal prediction
extends the framework to covariate shift when appropriate likelihood ratios or a
weighted-exchangeability structure are available
\cite{tibshirani2019covariate}. Adaptive Conformal Inference updates a control
parameter online and targets long-run coverage under changing distributions
\cite{gibbs2021adaptive}. Other work analyzes conformal prediction beyond
exchangeability and non-exchangeable conformal risk control
\cite{barber2023beyond,farinhas2024nexcrc}.

NCPNET combines score diffusion and efficiency-aware optimization for
non-exchangeable temporal graph neural networks \cite{wang2025ncpnet}. Its node
and temporal-graph benchmarks differ from event-KG entity completion, but its
design is a relevant temporal comparison rather than generic background. These
methods motivate recent-history weighting, but they do not imply that an
arbitrary exponential time decay is formally valid for TKG forecasting. We use
ACI and non-exchangeable methods as conceptual reference points and explicitly
label the proposed rule as empirical calibration. The implemented weighted
quantile is therefore not described as having a guarantee that it does not
mathematically satisfy.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Temporal Graph and Query Semantics}

Let \(\mathcal{E}\) be a closed entity vocabulary and \(\mathcal{R}\) a relation
set. A temporal knowledge graph is a set of observed quadruples
\[
  \mathcal{G}=\{(s,r,o,t):s,o\in\mathcal{E},\,r\in\mathcal{R},\,t\in\mathcal{T}\}.
\]
An object query is \(x=(s,r,?,t)\). A subject query is converted to
\((o,r^{-1},?,t)\) using an explicit inverse relation, allowing both directions
to share the same scoring and calibration interface.

For a query \(x\), let \(Y_t(x)\subseteq\mathcal{E}\) be all entities recorded
as valid answers at timestamp \(t\). The primary unit is an observed
query--label pair, so label-marginal coverage is
\[
  \widehat{\cov}_{\mathrm{label}}
  =\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(x,y)}\mathbb{1}\{y\in C_t(x)\}.
\]
Two stricter query-level quantities are full-set coverage,
\(\mathbb{1}\{Y_t(x)\subseteq C_t(x)\}\), and partial answer recall,
\[
  \frac{|Y_t(x)\cap C_t(x)|}{|Y_t(x)|}.
\]
This distinction is necessary because covering a randomly indexed observed
label is weaker than covering every recorded answer to a multi-answer query.
The confirmatory run reports both label-marginal coverage and these query-level
diagnostics. The latter are computed by grouping observed labels that share the
same seed, deletion rate, method, prediction side, timestamp, subject, and
relation.

The study is a closed-world, transductive temporal extrapolation experiment:
the entity vocabulary is constructed from the complete benchmark, although
facts from future timestamps are not available to the scorer or calibrator.
The implementation can flag entities absent from training for future-only
diagnostics, but the present ICEWS14 analysis does not use that flag as a
reported result. We do not claim inductive forecasting unless a backbone can
construct new-entity representations from attributes or revealed neighborhoods.

\subsection{Temporal Roles and Information Boundary}

Unique timestamps are sorted and split into the earliest 60\% for scorer
training, the next 20\% for development and calibration, and the final 20\% for
testing. No timestamp crosses a boundary. The development/calibration interval
is further divided, in temporal order, into 25\% scorer validation, 30\%
calibrator tuning, 15\% optional selector diagnostics, and 30\% final initial
calibration. Examples used for model or optional selector choice do not re-enter
the final calibration pool.

At test timestamp \(t\), the model first scores \emph{all} object and subject
queries. A single batch-level calibration state produces all sets at that
timestamp. Only after those predictions have been recorded are the observed
labels revealed and appended to the pool. The primary experiment assumes that a
completed timestamp batch is available before the next timestamp batch. A
delayed-feedback audit additionally withholds completed test batches for
1, 3, or 7 extra timestamp blocks before their scores can enter the rolling
calibration pool.

\subsection{Scores, Nonconformity, and Sets}

Let \(f_\theta(x,y)\) be a score assigned by a frozen TKG backbone. The
implemented scorer is a continuous-time DistMult model, whose simplified
four-way score is
\[
  f_\theta(s,r,o,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}
  e_{s,k}e_{r,k}e_{o,k}e_{\tau(t),k},
\]
where \(e_{\tau(t)}\) is generated by shared polynomial and sinusoidal features
rather than by a separate embedding for each future timestamp. DistMult
provides a transparent and exactly enumerable backbone for studying calibration
over the full entity vocabulary \cite{yang2015distmult}. The paper therefore
does not claim that the same effect has been established for recurrent graph
backbones such as RE-GCN.

The margin nonconformity score for candidate \(y\) is
\[
  a(x,y)=\max_{y'\in\mathcal{E}}f_\theta(x,y')-f_\theta(x,y).
\]
Given a threshold \(q_t\), the prediction set is
\[
  C_t(x)=\{y\in\mathcal{E}:a(x,y)\leq q_t\}.
\]
The highest-scoring candidate always has score zero and is included for any
nonnegative threshold. Consequently, empty-set rate is not informative for this
score. We instead report singleton rate and normalized set size
\(|C_t(x)|/|\mathcal{E}|\). Rank-based and softmax-mass scores are useful
diagnostics because margin scale may vary across backbones; the experiments
below include rank and APS/RAPS shortlist variants as empirical utility checks.

\section{Prequential Calibration Protocol}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Implemented Method}

The implemented protocol is an empirical, model-agnostic calibration layer for
timestamp-batched TKG forecasting. It consumes frozen all-entity score vectors
and never updates the scorer during testing. For each test timestamp it computes
prediction sets using only calibration scores revealed before that timestamp.
The primary comparison is static calibration versus rolling prequential
calibration. Fixed recency weighting and a calibration-only half-life selector are
included as diagnostic variants for studying whether weighting within the
rolling pool changes the reliability--utility trade-off. Rank-threshold and
validation-selected APS/RAPS rolling shortlists are evaluated after the main
reliability comparison as utility diagnostics for reducing candidate-set size.

\subsection{Static and Rolling Empirical Calibration}

Let \(\{a_i\}_{i=1}^{n}\) be initial calibration scores and let
\(\alpha=0.10\). Static KGCP uses the finite-sample order statistic with index
\[
  k=\min\{n,\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil\}
\]
and keeps its threshold fixed throughout the test stream. Under exchangeability,
this construction has the usual marginal interpretation; our temporally ordered
test does not automatically satisfy that assumption.

Rolling calibration uses the most recent \(W=1000\) revealed scores and
recomputes an equal-weight empirical threshold at every timestamp. It is the
primary diagnostic method because it isolates the value of recent-history
prequential updating from additional weighting or half-life selection machinery.
Here \(W\) counts nonconformity scores, not timestamp blocks. Because batch
sizes vary, the real temporal span of a fixed score window is a diagnostic
quantity rather than a design constant; Section~\ref{sec:results} reports this
span explicitly.

\subsection{Rank and Mass Shortlist Diagnostics}

The margin threshold can return very large sets when many candidates have scores
close to the top score. We therefore audit two shortlist families using the
same prequential information boundary. First, rank rolling replaces the margin
score by the one-based rank of the true label under the frozen scorer. The
threshold is a rolling empirical top-\(k\) value computed from strictly past
true-label ranks, and the prediction set is the corresponding top-\(k\)
shortlist.

Second, APS/RAPS rolling computes a softmax distribution over all entities for
each query, using softmax temperature \(T=1\), and uses cumulative sorted
probability mass as a nonconformity score. RAPS candidates add a penalty
\(\lambda\max(r-k,0)\) after rank \(k\). The fixed candidate grid contains APS
and RAPS with \(k\in\{50,100,250,500,1000\}\) and
\(\lambda\in\{10^{-4},5\times10^{-4},10^{-3},2\times10^{-3}\}\), giving 21
candidate score rules. Selection is performed only on calibration-validation
batches by choosing the smallest mean set size among candidates whose
observed-label coverage is within 0.02 of the target. The selected candidate is
then evaluated on the test stream with rolling thresholds over strictly past
scores. These variants are reported as empirical reliability--utility
diagnostics, not as new distribution-free guarantees for non-exchangeable
temporal data.

\subsection{Fixed Drift-Weighted Calibration}

For a score observed at real time \(\tau_i<\tau_t\), fixed decay assigns
\[
  w_i(t;h)=2^{-(\tau_t-\tau_i)/h},
  \qquad h\in\mathcal{H}=\{7,14,30,\infty\}.
\]
Within the rolling pool \(\mathcal{P}_t\), weights are normalized and the
inclusive empirical threshold is
\[
  q_t(h)=\inf\left\{u:
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t}\bar{w}_i(t;h)\,
  \mathbb{1}\{a_i\leq u\}\geq 1-\alpha\right\}.
\]
The \(h=\infty\) candidate is equal weighting \emph{within} the rolling pool,
not an all-history static method. For ICEWS14, one dense timestamp corresponds
to one day. Other datasets must retain real elapsed time so that a half-life is
not changed by timestamp reindexing.

Fixed Drift-Weighted calibration compares candidate half-lives once on a
temporally ordered pre-test calibration split. The selected value \(h^\star\)
is then fixed globally, so every test batch uses \(q_t(h^\star)\).

\subsection{Exploratory Half-Life Features}

The exploratory half-life selector computes at timestamp \(t\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
  z_t=\big[&\TV(p_t(r),p_{\mathrm{ref}}(r)),
  \KS(m_t,m_{\mathrm{ref}}),\\
  &1-\Rec_t,\ \log(1+n_t)\big]^{\top}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(p_t(r)\) is the relation distribution of current queries;
\(m_t\) is the empirical distribution of each query's top-1 minus top-2 score
margin; \(\Rec_t\) is the fraction of object queries \((s,r)\), or inverse
subject queries \((o,r^{-1})\), seen in the revealed deployment history since
the final initial calibration interval; and
\(n_t\) is the number of queries in the batch. The reference distributions and
feature standardization statistics are fixed using the calibrator-tuning
interval. Current labels are not features.

\subsection{Exploratory Half-Life Selector}

For every tuning timestamp and every \(h\in\mathcal{H}\), the implementation
simulates the prequential threshold using only earlier scores and records
observed-label coverage \(c_{t,h}\) and log normalized set size \(s_{t,h}\).
For each half-life, two ridge regressors estimate
\[
  \widehat{c}_h(z_t)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \widehat{s}_h(z_t).
\]
The ridge coefficient is fixed by the resolved configuration and fitted only on
pre-test timestamps. At test time, if one or more candidates satisfy
\(\widehat{c}_h(z_t)\geq 0.90\), the selector chooses the candidate with minimum
\(\widehat{s}_h(z_t)\). Otherwise it chooses maximum predicted coverage.
Remaining ties are broken deterministically by predicted size and the candidate
order in the resolved configuration.

The selected \(h_t\) produces one inclusive threshold for the complete
timestamp batch. The labels are then revealed, margins are added to the pool,
and the state is checkpointed. Because the present results show only negligible
differences between this selector and rolling/fixed recency baselines, it is
interpreted as an ablation rather than as the main empirical claim.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:riskcal} summarizes the complete protocol.

\subsection{Controlled History-Missingness Interventions}

History interventions modify positive training facts only; development,
calibration, and test facts remain fixed. The primary mechanism is
\emph{entity-coverage-constrained uniform deletion}: a seeded uniform mask is
drawn at rates \(\rho\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3\}\), but each entity's earliest
training fact is retained if the mask would otherwise remove every training
occurrence. Since retention depends on entity and time, this mechanism is not
missing completely at random.

The experiment in this paper uses this primary mechanism only. We
reserve unconstrained independent deletion, contiguous temporal-block deletion,
recent-history-biased deletion, and relation-family dropout for external
validity studies because they require separate preregistration and masking
audits. Every reported mechanism--rate--seed condition retrains the scorer once;
all calibrators share that checkpoint and its candidate scores.

\subsection{Claim Boundary and Complexity}

The weighted and adaptive variants are empirical calibration rules. The age
weights are not treated as covariate-shift density ratios, the threshold does
not allocate test-point mass at \(+\infty\), and no randomization is used for
ties. We therefore do not claim finite-sample coverage under arbitrary drift.
Formal baselines retain only the guarantees established under their own
assumptions.

For \(B\) queries, \(E=|\mathcal{E}|\) candidate entities, dense embedding
dimension \(d\), and pool size \(W\), the implemented vectorized DistMult scorer
performs \(O(BEd)\) arithmetic and materializes \(O(BE)\) scores. Sorting a
calibration pool costs \(O(W\log W)\) per threshold without incremental data
structures. Drift-feature computation is linear in the batch plus relation
vocabulary. In the confirmatory ICEWS14 run, peak CUDA memory remained close to
1.05 GB and the mean per-condition wall time was 203.6 s for training, 13.0 s
for calibration, and 45.4 s for inference.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evidence Classes and Admissible Claims}

We keep evidence classes separate. Unit and smoke verification check software
behavior but supply no dataset-level empirical result. Local CPU diagnostics are
engineering checks only and do not populate paper claims. The results
reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} come from a frozen confirmatory run over
the full ICEWS14 split with five seeds, four deletion rates, immutable
manifests, and condition-level completion markers. The run supports a
prequential calibration diagnostic, not a claim that the half-life selector or
shortlist variants solve temporal KG forecasting.

\subsection{Datasets and Temporal Splits}

ICEWS14 contains daily political event quadruples and is used for the
main analysis. The benchmark is temporally sorted before splitting:
the earliest 60\% of timestamps train the scorer, the next 20\% support
validation and calibration roles, and the final 20\% form the test stream.

\subsection{Backbones and Calibration Baselines}

The implemented scorer is continuous-time DistMult. Its time representation is
generated by shared polynomial and sinusoidal features, so it can score future
timestamps without allocating a separate embedding for each time ID. A simple
Frequency ranking baseline is computed from the retained training facts and
serves as a simple reference for the scorer. Exactly the same score tensors are
supplied to:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item uncalibrated Top-1;
  \item Static empirical calibration;
  \item equal-weight Rolling, the primary prequential recalibration baseline;
  \item Fixed Drift-Weighted;
  \item an exploratory half-life selector;
  \item rank-threshold rolling shortlists;
  \item APS rolling and validation-selected RAPS rolling shortlists.
\end{enumerate}
Static empirical calibration is the direct split-calibration analogue of the
static KGCP setting, but our temporally ordered evaluation does not transfer its
exchangeability guarantee. Predicate-conditional KGCP, ACI, and
non-exchangeable risk-control variants remain important external baselines for
future work; they are not claimed as completed comparisons in this diagnostic
study.

\subsection{Metrics}

Ranking quality is measured by filtered MRR and Hits@1/3/10, macro-averaged over
subject and object prediction. Filtering uses other observed answers at the same
timestamp for offline metric computation only; those labels never update the
calibrator before the timestamp batch is complete.

Reliability metrics include label-marginal micro-query coverage, macro-time
coverage, positive undercoverage \((0.90-\widehat{\cov})_+\), absolute coverage
gap, and window-level lower confidence bounds. Raw minimum single-timestamp
coverage is descriptive because it is sensitive to small batch sizes. Query-level
full-set coverage and partial answer recall are reported separately for
multi-answer semantics. The diagnostic also records the fraction of unique
queries with multiple recorded answers and the maximum number of recorded
answers for any query.
Relation-side diagnostics aggregate observed-label coverage by original
relation ID and prediction side after mapping inverse subject-prediction
relations back to their base relation. Only relation-side groups with at least
250 observed labels across all seeds and all five seeds represented are used in
the worst-group audit. Because seeds repeat the same ICEWS14 test timeline, this
threshold is an audit support filter, not a count of independent natural
samples. These statistics are conditional diagnostics, not formal conditional
coverage guarantees.

Efficiency metrics include mean, median, and P90 set size; normalized size;
singleton rate; and calibration, inference, memory, and storage overhead.
The diagnostic outputs also record fixed set-size caps, answer rate,
unconditional full-set recall, candidate load, rank shortlists, and APS/RAPS
shortlists. The main text emphasizes coverage, full-set query coverage,
partial answer recall, mean and tail set size, runtime, and memory because
these quantities define the reported reliability--utility envelope.

\subsection{Primary Estimand and Inference}

The unique primary setting is ICEWS14, continuous-time DistMult, 30\%
entity-coverage-constrained uniform deletion, and target coverage 0.90. The
primary estimand is
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{roll}}
  &=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|\,|\mathcal{T}|}
    \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} d_{s,t},\\
  d_{s,t}
  &=(0.90-\widehat{\cov}^{\,s,t}_{\mathrm{Static}})_+\\
  &\quad -(0.90-\widehat{\cov}^{\,s,t}_{\mathrm{Rolling}})_+ .
\end{aligned}
\]
This estimand measures how much recent-history prequential recalibration reduces
timestamp-macro positive undercoverage relative to a static threshold. It is not
the same as taking the positive part after averaging micro-query coverage over
the full test set. The exploratory half-life selector is evaluated against
Rolling and Fixed Drift-Weighted as an ablation; rank and APS/RAPS shortlists
are evaluated as utility diagnostics. Other datasets, backbones, missingness
mechanisms, and external conformal baselines are secondary extensions outside
this diagnostic run.

The scorer uses five seeds
\(\{17,29,43,59,71\}\), selected before observing the complete confirmatory
result. Inference uses a paired seed-resampled circular moving-block bootstrap
with 20,000 replicates. Each replicate resamples the five seeds with replacement
and draws one shared set of contiguous seven-timestamp blocks, with circular
wrapping, that is applied to all sampled seeds. The same scheme is used for the
primary Static-to-Rolling undercoverage reduction and for the scorer-vs.-
Frequency validity test. One-sided \(p\)-values use
\((\#\{\Delta^\ast\leq 0\}+1)/(B+1)\).
The seed-wise undercoverage reductions are 0.0735, 0.0624, 0.0705, 0.0771,
and 0.0778 for seeds 17, 29, 43, 59, and 71; the full seed-wise table is
included in the diagnostic outputs.
We additionally report a block-length sensitivity analysis with block lengths
\(3,7,14,\) and \(21\), holding the estimand, seed-resampling scheme, and
number of bootstrap replicates fixed.

\subsection{Implementation and Reproducibility}

The current code records resolved configuration, environment, dataset
fingerprint, raw-file hashes, deletion-mask audit, checkpoint identity,
condition-level outputs, resource use, and atomic final artifacts. Interrupted
conditions resume only when configuration, data, mask, and checkpoint hashes
match. Completed runs are immutable.
The confirmatory run used PyTorch 2.5.1+cu124 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090.
It completed 20 seed--deletion conditions in 88.4 minutes, writing 7,300
per-window rows and 3,859,600 per-query rows.
The same data directory stores query-level, timestamp-block, history-baseline,
window-ablation, pool, half-life, deletion-interaction, delayed-feedback, and
relation-side diagnostics. These files reuse frozen checkpoints from the
confirmatory run and audit the calibration layer without retraining the
scorer.

\subsection{Window and Half-Life Diagnostics}

The window audit explains why Fixed Weighted and Selected half-life are close to Rolling. The

decay is nearly unidentified in this short score-count window.

\subsection{Delayed-Feedback Sensitivity}

The main protocol updates the rolling pool after a complete timestamp batch is
predicted and evaluated. This is optimistic for settings where labels arrive
late. We therefore rerun the rolling calibration audit from the same frozen
checkpoints while withholding completed test batches for 1, 3, or 7 additional
timestamp blocks. Table~\ref{tab:delay-feedback} shows that delay weakens

Static-to-Rolling undercoverage reduction remains positive under this stress
test, but its observed value decreases from 0.0723 to 0.0637. We interpret this
as evidence that the rolling result depends on timely feedback, while the
reported ICEWS14 conclusion is not eliminated by short additional delays.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Accuracy, Reliability, and Utility}

The study separates three questions. Ranking asks whether the observed answer
is ordered above alternatives; coverage asks whether it is included in the
candidate set; utility asks how many candidates must be retained. The
confirmatory run shows that these questions can disagree. Static calibration
under-covers, while rolling calibration restores near-target average
observed-label coverage. The same rolling threshold returns large sets: at
30\% deletion, the label-weighted mean set size is 3,802.5 entities, the
unique-query full-vocabulary rate is 36.5\%, and the weakest eligible
relation-side group remains at 0.7625 observed-label coverage. The method is
therefore a reliability repair for a closed-vocabulary ranking system, not a
conditional-coverage guarantee.

\subsection{Shortlist Trade-offs}

The shortlist diagnostics sharpen the utility picture. Naive hard caps reduce
candidate load but lose many answerable queries; at a 5,000-entity cap, rolling
calibration answers only 45.8\% of unique queries and retains 0.3605
unconditional full-set recall. Rank rolling and validation-selected RAPS rolling
are more informative operating points because they change the nonconformity
scale before thresholding. Rank rolling removes full-vocabulary outputs and
reduces tail size, but its full-set coverage is lower than margin rolling.
Validation-selected RAPS rolling reduces mean and median size further at the
0.90 target, but its P90 size is worse than rank rolling and its advantage over
margin is not supported at target 0.92. The empirical message is not that one
shortlist rule dominates, but that reliability must be evaluated together with
answer-set semantics and set-size cost.

\subsection{Limits of Half-Life Adaptation}

The half-life diagnostic variant uses relation total variation, score-margin KS
distance, recurrence, and batch size as drift signals. In this ICEWS14 run it
does not improve meaningfully over Rolling or Fixed Weighted calibration. The
window audit explains why: the default 1,000-score pool spans only two
timestamp blocks at the median, so candidate half-lives have nearly the same
effective sample size. Stronger claims about feature-driven half-life selection
would require longer real-time pools, additional datasets, selector ablations,
and checks of half-life prediction accuracy.

\subsection{Coverage on Event KGs}

ICEWS and GDELT are built from observed event records, whose labels may be
incomplete or noisy. Thus 90\% coverage means 90\% coverage of
\emph{recorded evaluation labels} under the stated protocol, not 90\% coverage
of all real-world true events or a guarantee for an individual query.

\subsection{Claim Boundary}

The completed run supports a bounded empirical claim: under the stated ICEWS14
protocol, static calibration under-covers and rolling prequential calibration
restores near-target average observed-label coverage, while shortlist variants
trace different points on the reliability--utility frontier. It does not
establish superiority over temporal conformal methods, recurrent TKG backbones,
or predicate-conditional calibrators. The window, sensitivity, delayed-feedback,
and relation-side audits further narrow the claim: no generally optimal window
length is identified, delayed feedback degrades coverage, target 0.92 weakens
the validation-selected RAPS utility claim, and the weakest relation-direction
slices still under-cover.

\section{Limitations and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Construct validity.}
Controlled deletion is a stress test, not a generative model of missing
knowledge. Entity-coverage-constrained deletion is not MCAR, so the primary
estimand concerns one specified mechanism.

\textbf{Internal validity.}
Temporal leakage can arise from reusing selector labels, revealing part of a
timestamp before predicting the rest, using future facts during prediction, or
tuning on test coverage. The four-role pre-test split, timestamp-batched
updates, resolved configuration, split manifest, condition hashes, and
completion markers audit these constraints.

\textbf{Statistical validity.}
Five seeds are limited for training-randomness inference. Timestamp
observations are autocorrelated, and answers to the same query are dependent.
The seven-timestamp circular moving-block bootstrap preserves local temporal
adjacency better than independent resampling but still makes assumptions. We
therefore report raw seed values, distinguish seed-level from temporal
uncertainty, and include block-length sensitivity in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.
The delayed-feedback audit changes only the time at which completed test
batches enter the calibration pool; it does not model noisy labels or missing
labels. The set-size cap audit is an abstention diagnostic, not a conformal
method for bounded candidate sets; abstained queries count as unanswered in the
unconditional recall metric. The relation-side audit has 76 eligible groups
after support filtering and is a targeted diagnostic, not a comprehensive
relation-conditional guarantee.

\textbf{External validity.}
The evidence comes from ICEWS14, a recurrent political-event benchmark with a
closed entity vocabulary. It does not establish behavior on non-event temporal
graphs, low-recurrence domains, inductive new-entity settings, or other
missing-history mechanisms. Prequential Repeat reaches MRR 0.3631 at 30\%
deletion, above the frozen scorer's 0.3105, which reinforces that this is not a
state-of-the-art ranking comparison.

\textbf{Scalability.}
For dense dimension \(d\), exact all-entity evaluation performs \(O(BEd)\)
score arithmetic and can materialize \(O(BE)\) candidate scores for \(B\)
queries and \(E\) entities. Approximate retrieval may omit the true entity
before calibration and therefore change the estimand. Any ANN result must be
accompanied by exact small-scale comparisons and candidate-recall audits.

\textbf{Guarantee boundary.}
The empirical rolling, weighted, and selected half-life thresholds have no finite-sample
distribution-free guarantee under arbitrary temporal drift. Calling them a
risk-control theorem would be incorrect. Formal baselines are interpreted only
under their published assumptions.

\textbf{Practical implications.}
For applications that need candidate answer sets rather than only ranked lists,
static early-window calibration should be audited after deployment-time drift.
Rolling prequential calibration is a useful diagnostic repair under the present
ICEWS14 protocol, but the resulting sets can be too large for direct human use.
Operational systems should therefore choose an operating point jointly over
observed-label coverage, full-set query coverage, feedback latency, subgroup
coverage, and candidate-set size rather than optimizing a single headline
coverage number.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies temporal KG forecasting through the lens of prequential
answer-set calibration. Under the stated ICEWS14 protocol, static calibration
under-covers later timestamps: at 30\% training-history deletion it covers
0.8235 of observed labels against a 0.90 target. Rolling prequential
calibration reaches 0.8998, with a paired timestamp-block undercoverage
reduction of 0.0723 (95\% CI [0.0535, 0.0938]). The result is a reliability
repair, not a ranking improvement: a prequential Repeat heuristic has higher
MRR than the frozen scorer in this benchmark.

The reliability gain also creates a utility problem. Rolling calibration
returns thousands of entities on average and often reaches the full vocabulary.
Hard caps reduce load but sharply reduce answerability and unconditional
full-set recall. Rank rolling and validation-selected RAPS rolling provide more
useful operating points. Rank rolling reduces tail size but lowers full-set
query coverage; validation-selected RAPS rolling reduces mean and median size
further at target 0.90 but does not dominate rank rolling in the tail and is
not supported as a margin-beating size reduction at target 0.92. Relation-side,
delayed-feedback, window, and half-life diagnostics confirm the same boundary:
recent-history calibration is useful, but its reliability must be interpreted
together with answer-set semantics, feedback latency, subgroup behavior, and
candidate-set cost. Future work should test stronger TKG backbones, additional
temporal graphs, longer calibration histories, other missing-history mechanisms,
and shortlist methods that reduce size while preserving query-level coverage.
